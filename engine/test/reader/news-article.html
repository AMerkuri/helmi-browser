Harbour ferry returns after storm repairs

By Mara Lind

The harbour ferry between Northpoint and the island of Vell resumed its timetable on Monday, six weeks after a winter storm tore the loading ramp from its hinges and grounded the vessel at the outer pier.

Repairs at the regional shipyard cost 1.2 million euros, according to the port authority, which says the new ramp is rated for waves twice as high as the one it replaces. The authority paid for the work from its maintenance reserve rather than raising fares.

During the closure, about 900 island residents relied on a smaller water taxi that ran three times a day. Shopkeepers on Vell said deliveries of fresh food had been cut by half, and the island school moved some lessons online.

Timetable changes

The ferry now leaves Northpoint at 7:10 instead of 7:30, so that commuters can reach the mainland rail connection. The last crossing of the day moves from 21:00 to 22:15 on Fridays and Saturdays during the summer season.

Captain Ilse Varn, who has worked on the route for nineteen years, said the crew had trained on the new ramp in calm water last week. "It handles much better in a crosswind," she said.

What comes next

The port authority plans to replace the ferry itself by 2029. A study of an electric vessel, charged at both piers overnight, is due to be published in the spring and will be discussed at a public meeting on the island.
